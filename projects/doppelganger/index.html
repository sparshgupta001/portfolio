Doppelgänger: Making Web Privacy Risks Understandable

How can a browser extension communicate invisible web tracking and third-party data risks to everyday users without causing alarm fatigue or relying on technical jargon?

01 · Project Overview

The Invisible Risk of Web Browsing

Everyday web browsing exposes users to aggressive trackers, third-party script fingerprinting, and session recording tools. While technical power-users can configure complex content blockers or inspect network requests via DevTools, the vast majority of non-technical people have no visibility into what is being observed, collected, or shared behind the scenes.

Core Design Hypothesis: If security and privacy risks are structured in calm, plain-language tiers with a single contextual action, everyday users will make safer browsing choices instead of immediately closing or ignoring alert dialogs.
02 · Problem Definition

The "Alarm Fatigue" Trap

Most privacy tools fail because of how they communicate. When every website triggers bright red banners with terrifying terms like "Potential Cross-Site Scripting Injection", users develop sensory fatigue. They click "Dismiss" or "Proceed Anyway" simply to continue their task.

Current Friction: Opaque Warnings

Browsers either hide tracking information completely inside nested setting menus, or throw catastrophic warnings that fail to explain what the user can realistically do about it.

The Design Objective

Create a persistent yet non-intrusive extension surface that delivers an instant status reading in under 3 seconds, reserving high-contrast cues strictly for actionable threats.

03 · Target Users & Context

Who Needs Calm Security?

Doppelgänger is designed for everyday internet users: students, remote workers, and online shoppers who care about their privacy but do not possess computer science or cyber-forensic backgrounds.

Context A: High-Intent Work & Reading

Users are researching or reading articles. They do not want modal popups interrupting reading flow, but value subtle peripheral reassurance that canvas fingerprinting is suppressed.

Context B: Transactional & Form Flows

Users are typing checkout or login credentials on unfamiliar domains. This is the critical moment where plain-language permission scrutiny is essential before submitting data.

05 · Key Design Challenges

Translating Forensic Metrics into Human Terms

Challenge 1: Progressive Disclosure

Balancing a glanceable summary with deep forensic audit data. Power users want to see exact script URLs, while everyday users only need to know if their session is safe.

Challenge 2: Accessible Status Indicators

Ensuring color is never the only signal. Red, yellow, and green status indicators are accompanied by distinct geometric icons (Shield, Triangle, Octagon) and plain-text tier tags.

08 · Design Decisions & Rationale

Why the Interface Works

1. Plain-Language Risk Tiers

Instead of exposing obscure HTTP headers or cipher suites, risks are categorized into three human states: Safe, Caution, and Action Required. This establishes immediate mental clarity.

2. Single Action Per Warning

Multiple buttons induce decision paralysis. Every Doppelgänger warning surfaces exactly one recommended primary action (e.g. "Shield Session" or "Block Trackers") alongside an expandable "Why?" toggle.

3. Progressive Disclosure Architecture

The first screen satisfies 95% of user inquiries in 2 seconds. Detailed audit logs, cookie scopes, and script origins are accessible via a secondary drawer for curious users.

4. Dual Non-Color Accessibility

To respect color-blind users and high-contrast preferences, every risk status couples color with an unambiguous icon and explicit uppercase text badge.
