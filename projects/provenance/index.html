01 · Project Overview

The Traceability Gap in Global Trade

Modern supply chains involve dozens of independent handoffs: raw material harvesting, regional processing, cross-border shipping, customs clearance, and local distribution. While enterprise ERP databases store massive quantities of ledger data, this information is fragmented across disparate databases, legacy spreadsheets, and paper certificates.

Core Design Challenge: Design a human-centered dashboard that translates dense custody records and environmental logs into a clear, trustworthy visual timeline that can be verified in seconds by inspectors and buyers.
02 · Problem Definition

Data Abundance, Zero Clarity

When quality inspectors or retail procurement managers audit a product batch, they are typically forced to cross-reference multiple disjointed PDF manifests and raw CSV logs.

1. The Verification Tax

Confirming that an organic coffee or fair-trade cocoa shipment genuinely originated from a verified farm co-op requires hours of manual cross-referencing between shipping bills and customs timestamps.

2. Opaque Custody Gaps

If a cold-chain failure occurs during ocean transit, identifying which freight custodian had custody at the exact moment of temperature compromise is notoriously difficult.

03 · Target Users & Context

Designing for Two Perspectives

Primary: Quality & Compliance Auditors

Inspectors who need to certify origin, cold-chain temperature thresholds, and tamper-evident seals before clearing shipments into regional distribution centers.

Secondary: Ethical Procurement Buyers

Brand managers and retailers who need consumer-facing transparency proof to substantiate fair-trade, organic, and sustainability marketing claims.

04 · Information Architecture

Structuring the 3-Tier Traceability Hierarchy

I restructured the raw supply chain database schema into an intuitive, 3-tier progressive disclosure hierarchy:

Tier 1: Instant Verdict (0–3 Seconds)

Prominent authenticity seal, batch identifier, origin country flag, and composite compliance status (e.g. "Verified Authentic · Cold Chain Maintained").

Tier 2: Custody Timeline (3–10 Seconds)

Chronological step-by-step chain of custody showing timestamps, geographic coordinates, custodian entity, and sensor telemetry highlights.

Tier 3: Cryptographic Proof (On Demand)

Expandable forensic layer revealing raw sensor logs, tamper seal hashes, inspection certificates, and sign-off digital signatures.

Design Rationale

Auditors scanning 40 batches an hour get immediate status clarity without losing the capability to deep-dive into forensic evidence when anomalies arise.

07 · Design Decisions & Rationale

Principles for Trustable Data

1. Color-Coded Sensor Limits

Sensor ranges (temperature, humidity, vibration) utilize calm contextual states. If a threshold is approached, a yellow warning flag signals where and when the deviation occurred.

2. Verifiable Entity Stamps

Each checkpoint lists the legal entity and individual inspector badge rather than vague system timestamps, establishing personal accountability across supply networks.

3. Print-Ready Audit Export

While digital dashboards are preferred, compliance agencies require physical paper trails. The layout was designed with CSS print media queries for clean one-page export.

4. Mobile-Responsive QR Landing

Designed a responsive layout down to 360px so dock workers can inspect batches directly from smartphone cameras while scanning warehouse pallets.

08 · Outcome & Future Iterations

Production Implementation & Roadmap

Provenance was built as a full-stack proof-of-concept using Python, Flask, MongoDB, and React.js. It demonstrated how thoughtful interaction design and information architecture can turn cumbersome supply-chain ledgers into transparent consumer trust.
